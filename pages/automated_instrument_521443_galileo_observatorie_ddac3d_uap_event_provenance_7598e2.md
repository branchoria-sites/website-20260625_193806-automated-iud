## Introduction

A short video of an unusual object in the sky is not, by itself, strong scientific evidence. For the [Galileo]({{ 'galileo/' | relative_url }}) Project’s observatory-based approach to unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), a candidate event becomes scientifically useful only when researchers can reconstruct exactly how the observation was produced: which sensors recorded it, how those sensors were calibrated, what software processed the data, and what assumptions entered the analysis. This complete record is known as provenance.

The emphasis on provenance reflects a recurring problem in UAP investigations. Many famous cases rely on limited recordings, incomplete metadata, or sensor systems whose configuration cannot be fully reconstructed afterwards. NASA’s independent UAP study concluded that analysis is frequently hindered by poor calibration, missing sensor metadata, insufficient measurements and inadequate [baseline data]({{ 'baseline-data/' | relative_url }}). The Galileo Project was designed specifically to overcome those weaknesses through continuous, instrumented observation and transparent data handling. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science+2NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportFor any scientific analysis purposes, including UAP analysis, it is essential that the data used...</span></span></span>

## What Provenance Adds Beyond a Video Clip

A video answers only a narrow question: what appears on the screen. Provenance answers a much larger set of questions that determine whether the image can support a scientific claim.

For a Galileo candidate event, provenance can include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Raw sensor outputs before later processing.
* Time stamps and synchronisation records.
* Camera settings such as exposure, gain and frame rate.
* Environmental conditions at the time of detection.
* Calibration files and instrument status records.
* Software versions and processing workflows.
* Information from other sensors observing the same event.

</div>

Without this chain of information, an apparent anomaly may be impossible to evaluate. A bright object could be a distant aircraft, a nearby insect, an optical reflection, a compression artefact, a tracking error or a genuinely unusual target. The image alone often cannot distinguish among these possibilities.

This concern is not unique to Galileo. NASA's UAP study highlighted that sensor metadata is essential because it provides context about acquisition conditions, instrument characteristics and limitations. The report noted that some apparent UAP cases were later shown to be sensor artefacts once calibration information and metadata were examined in detail. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AUAP_Independent_Study_Team_-_Final_Report.pdf/15" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:UAP Independent Study Team</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:UAP Independent Study Team - Final Report.pdf/1512 Nov 2023 — Indeed, several apparent UAP have been demonstrated to be se...</span></span></span>

In other words, provenance transforms an observation from a visual curiosity into a traceable measurement.

## How Raw Data and Calibration Records Support Review

The Galileo Project's observatory model is built around collecting data that can be independently reviewed rather than simply viewed. Its public descriptions repeatedly emphasise calibrated instruments, transparent analysis and scientifically controlled data acquisition. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://galileo.hsites.harvard.edu/activities" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: galileo.hsites.harvard.edu">[Galileo Project+2The Crimson]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">galileo.hsites.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Galileo Project Activities &#124; The Galileo Project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Galileo ProjectActivities &#124; The Galileo Project - Harvard UniversityThe Galileo Project research group will aim to identify the nature of...</span></span></span>

Calibration records are particularly important because they establish what the instrument was actually capable of measuring at the moment of the event.

Consider a hypothetical fast-moving target recorded by an infrared camera. To evaluate the observation, investigators need to know:

* Whether the camera's geometry was accurately calibrated.
* Whether the [pointing]({{ 'pointing/' | relative_url }}) system was functioning correctly.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Whether the detector had known noise patterns.
* Whether atmospheric conditions affected the signal.
* Whether other sensors observed the same target.

</div>

Galileo's published instrumentation work illustrates this philosophy. During commissioning of its all-sky infrared camera array, the project described calibration procedures that used [known aircraft]({{ 'known-aircraft/' | relative_url }}) positions from [ADS-B]({{ 'ads-b/' | relative_url }}) transponder data to establish reliable instrument performance. Such calibration allows later measurements to be interpreted quantitatively rather than impressionistically. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07956" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commissioning An All-Sky Infrared Camera Array for Detection Of Airborne ObjectsNovember 12, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2024</span></span></span>

[Raw data]({{ 'raw-data/' | relative_url }}) are equally important. Processed images may contain filtering, enhancement, object detection or machine-learning classifications. Those products can be useful, but scientific [review]({{ 'review/' | relative_url }}) requires access to the underlying observations so that independent researchers can determine whether the processing introduced distortions or assumptions.

The distinction mirrors broader scientific practice. In astronomy, particle physics and Earth observation, major claims are expected to be traceable back to original measurements. Galileo applies the same standard to UAP observations.

## Why Traceability Changes the Burden of Evidence

Most public UAP debates begin with interpretation. Observers see an unusual image and immediately ask what it represents.

Provenance changes the order of operations.

Instead of starting with a claim and searching for support, investigators begin with a documented measurement chain and evaluate what explanations remain plausible. This shifts the burden of evidence from subjective interpretation to reproducible analysis.

A provenance-rich event allows reviewers to ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Can the measurement be reproduced from the stored data?
* Can the object's position and motion be independently calculated?
* Were the instruments functioning normally?
* Do multiple sensors support the same conclusion?
* Could a known artefact explain the observation?

</div>

When these questions can be answered, confidence in the result increases regardless of whether the explanation is mundane or unusual.

NASA's independent study repeatedly stressed that future progress depends on systematic calibration, multiple measurements and thorough sensor metadata. The reason is straightforward: extraordinary interpretations become less dependent on trust and more dependent on verifiable evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA+2Sci.News: Breaking Science News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">update nasa shares uap independent study report names director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report14 Sept 2023 — We found that NASA can help the whole-of-government UAP effort through...</span></span></span>

## Why Galileo Prioritises Its Own Data

One of the clearest demonstrations of the project's commitment to provenance is its decision not to rely on unsolicited public photos and videos. The Galileo Project states that it is not accepting outside UAP media because it wants data that are reliable, consistent, calibrated and comparable. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://galileo.hsites.harvard.edu/FAQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: galileo.hsites.harvard.edu">[Galileo Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">galileo.hsites.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">high-quality and reliable, consistent, calibrated, and comparable.Read more...</span></span></span>

This policy is sometimes misunderstood as dismissing eyewitness reports. In reality, it reflects a methodological choice. Most externally supplied footage arrives without a complete record of sensor settings, calibration state, processing history or environmental context. Even when the imagery is authentic, the evidential chain is often incomplete.

By collecting observations through its own observatories, Galileo can preserve provenance from the moment of acquisition. Every step—from detection to storage to analysis—can be documented and reviewed.

That approach aligns with a broader consensus emerging across scientific and governmental UAP investigations: [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }}) cases often remain unresolved not because they are necessarily extraordinary, but because the information needed to evaluate them was never collected in the first place. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AUAP_Independent_Study_Team_-_Final_Report.pdf/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource+2Space]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:UAP Independent Study Team</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:UAP Independent Study Team - Final Report.pdf/512 Nov 2023 — At present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor c...</span></span></span>

## A Candidate Event Is Only as Strong as Its Audit Trail

Within the Galileo observatory framework, provenance is not administrative paperwork attached to a detection. It is part of the detection itself.

A candidate event gains scientific value when investigators can reconstruct the complete path from photons arriving at a sensor to the final interpretation presented in a report. The more complete that audit trail, the easier it becomes to identify errors, rule out artefacts, compare results across observatories and allow independent scrutiny.

For a field long dominated by ambiguous clips and incomplete records, provenance is what turns a sighting into evidence. Galileo's central wager is that if genuinely unusual aerial phenomena exist, the strongest case for them will come not from the most dramatic video, but from the observation whose origin, calibration and analysis can be traced step by step from beginning to end. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv+2World Scientific]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Galileo Project Observatory Class System Architecture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Galileo Project Observatory Class System ArchitectureMay 30, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 30, 2025</span></span></span>
