## Introduction

GREMLIN is often described as a sensor system, but its value depends just as much on the network of organisations that [review]({{ 'review/' | relative_url }}) what those sensors collect. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) was created because many UAP cases could not be resolved from a single video, [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}) return, or witness account. As GREMLIN expands government collection of instrumented data, AARO increasingly relies on research laboratories, aerospace specialists, intelligence organisations, military operators, civil aviation authorities, and scientific partners to determine whether an event is unusual or merely misunderstood. The result is a review process that is broader and more technically rigorous than traditional UFO investigations, but also more complex and sometimes slower.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Nov/14/2003583603/-1/-1/0/FY24-CONSOLIDATED-ANNUAL-REPORT-ON-UAP-508.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War+2AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FY24 CONSOLIDATED ANNUAL REPORT ON UAP 508</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarFiscal Year 2024 Consolidated Annual Report on...Nov 14, 2024 — The GREMLIN sensor suite contains several sensing...</span></span></span>

## Who Checks GREMLIN's UAP Evidence?

GREMLIN was developed with support from the Georgia Tech Research Institute (GTRI), a major defence research organisation with extensive experience in radar, sensing systems, signal processing, and multi-sensor data fusion. Public reporting and AARO documentation identify GTRI as a principal contributor to the sensor architecture behind GREMLIN.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://breakingdefense.com/2024/11/gremlin-but-no-aliens-pentagon-uap-office-plans-first-deployment-of-new-sensor-suite/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: breakingdefense.com">[Breaking Defense]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">breakingdefense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Breaking DefenseGREMLIN, but no aliens: Pentagon UAP office plans first...Nov 14, 2024 — GREMLIN is a network of 2D and 3D radar, and lo...</span></span></span>

This matters because a modern UAP case rarely involves a single piece of evidence. A GREMLIN deployment may generate radar tracks, infrared imagery, optical observations, radio-frequency measurements, aircraft transponder data, [timing]({{ 'timing/' | relative_url }}) information, and environmental records. Determining whether those streams describe the same object requires specialised expertise in sensor physics and data integration. Organisations such as GTRI provide capabilities that go beyond simple observation, helping investigators determine whether an apparent anomaly originates from an object in the environment, a sensor artefact, a software issue, or an ordinary aircraft seen under unusual conditions. The practical effect is that GREMLIN evidence enters a technical review pipeline rather than being judged solely by eyewitness testimony or isolated imagery. Sensor engineers can examine calibration issues, radar specialists can evaluate track quality, and analysts can compare multiple sensor outputs against known aviation and aerospace activity. This increases the chances of identifying conventional explanations before a case is classified as [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Nov/14/2003583603/-1/-1/0/FY24-CONSOLIDATED-ANNUAL-REPORT-ON-UAP-508.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FY24 CONSOLIDATED ANNUAL REPORT ON UAP 508</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarFiscal Year 2024 Consolidated Annual Report on...Nov 14, 2024 — The GREMLIN sensor suite contains several sensing...</span></span></span>

## Why Labs and Aerospace Partners Matter

One of the recurring findings in official UAP reports is that many incidents lack sufficient information for confident attribution. AARO's response has been to widen the number of institutions that can contribute expertise. Official reports describe a "whole-of-government" approach involving defence, intelligence, scientific, and aviation partners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/Unclassified-2022-Annual-Report-UAP.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified 2022 Annual Report UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National Intelligence2022 Annual Report on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena25 Jun 2021 — ODNI and AARO are committed to the resp...</span></span></span>

Laboratories and aerospace specialists contribute in several distinct ways:

* **Sensor validation:** determining whether unusual behaviour reflects an external object or a limitation of the observing system.
* **Trajectory analysis:** reconstructing motion from radar and optical data.
* **Signature analysis:** comparing observed characteristics against [known aircraft]({{ 'known-aircraft/' | relative_url }}), drones, balloons, satellites, and atmospheric phenomena.
* **Materials and technical assessment:** evaluating any physical evidence or technical indicators that may accompany a report.
* **Data fusion:** combining information from multiple sources into a single analytical picture. The aerospace dimension is particularly important because many UAP reports originate near military operations, restricted airspace, or sensitive facilities. An object that initially appears unexplained may ultimately match the performance profile of a known drone, aircraft, balloon, or orbital object once aerospace experts compare the data against flight records and technical databases. AARO's publicly released case assessments frequently show this pattern, where detailed analysis leads to identifications that were not obvious from the original imagery alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryAARO assesses, with high confidence, that the footage depicts the presence of a physical object. In governance terms, partner organisations act as independent technical checkpoints. Instead of relying on a single investigative office, the evidence can be reviewed by specialists whose expertise lies in the specific technologies involved.

## How Interagency Review Changes the Outcome

GREMLIN's sensor network is intended to collect better data, but the partner network determines how that data is interpreted. AARO works with organisations across the defence and intelligence communities as well as civil aviation authorities. Official briefings note cooperation with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), intelligence agencies, military commands, and scientific partners. NASA's UAP study also recommended formal liaison structures between NASA and AARO to improve information exchange and scientific review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The US Defense Department &amp; The UAP Mission</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The US Defense Department &amp; The UAP MissionJune 20, 2025 — AARO received more than 100 reports from the FAA, contributing to analyses...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

Interagency review changes evidence assessment in several ways.

**It reduces military-only bias.** Civil aviation data introduces observations from commercial pilots and civilian airspace, expanding the range of comparisons available to investigators. AARO reports that FAA reporting has already contributed significantly to UAP trend analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The US Defense Department &amp; The UAP Mission</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The US Defense Department &amp; The UAP MissionJune 20, 2025 — AARO received more than 100 reports from the FAA, contributing to analyses...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

**It increases access to reference data.** Intelligence and defence organisations possess information about classified systems, known foreign platforms, and operational activities that may explain an event. A case that appears anomalous from one perspective may become understandable when additional datasets are available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/Unclassified-2022-Annual-Report-UAP.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified 2022 Annual Report UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National Intelligence2022 Annual Report on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena25 Jun 2021 — ODNI and AARO are committed to the resp...</span></span></span>

**It creates standardised review procedures.** AARO reports describe efforts to establish common reporting and analytical practices across agencies. ## How Cross-Agency Review Can Strengthen Disclosure

Supporters of the partner-network model argue that it improves credibility. When multiple organisations independently examine the same evidence, conclusions become more defensible than those based on a single office's judgement.

In many instances, a case remains unresolved because the available data are incomplete, contradictory, or insufficient for confident attribution.
